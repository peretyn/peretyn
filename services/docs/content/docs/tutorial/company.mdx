---
title: 5. The company
description: Give a company's admins access to every document the company owns, and keep one company's admins out of another company's documents.
---

Four months in, your second customer signs up: Globex. You celebrate for about an hour, until a message from Acme lands in your inbox. Ivan, Acme's IT admin, needs to be able to open any Acme document: for audits, for legal requests, for the day someone leaves the company and their work has to be found.

You read the request again and stop at two words: _any Acme document_. Last week, that would have been the same as "any document". Since this morning, it isn't.

## Acme's IT admin

Your first instinct is a role on the user, checked before anything else:

```ts
if (user.role === 'admin') {
  return;
}
```

You type it, and then your hands stop above the keyboard. Whose admin? With Globex on board, that line would let Globex's admin open Acme's roadmap. The last line of `canViewDocument`, back in your first week, had to compare `user.companyId` with `document.companyId` for exactly this reason. It's the kind of comparison that's easy to get right once, and easy to forget in the next handler.

What Acme is asking for isn't "admins can see everything". It's "the admins of a company can see that company's documents". That's two facts and one rule:

- A <Term name="fact">fact</Term>: Ivan is an admin of Acme.
- Another fact: the roadmap belongs to Acme.
- A <Term name="rule">rule</Term>: the admins of a document's company can view it.

So the company becomes a type of its own, with admins:

```ts
organization: type({
  relations: { admin: ['user'] },
}),
```

Each document learns which company it belongs to, through a new <Term name="relation">relation</Term>:

```ts
organization: ['organization'],
```

And `can_view` gets one more way in:

```ts
can_view: any('viewer', 'can_edit', via('parent', 'can_view'), via('organization', 'admin')),
```

It's the same `via` you used for folders, pointed somewhere new: follow the document to its organization, and ask whether this user is an admin there.

Every document now needs to know its company. From now on, your app writes that tuple whenever a document is uploaded, right next to the owner. For the documents that already exist, you write it once: the roadmap, the launch plan and the announcement. Then you make Ivan an admin of Acme:

<include>../../../snippets/tutorial/05-company/admin.ts</include>

The script prints:

```txt
ivan can_view document:roadmap: allowed
ivan can_view document:launch-plan: allowed
ivan can_edit document:roadmap: denied
```

Ivan can open the roadmap, and even the launch plan, tucked away in the Leadership folder where only Alice could see it. The organization is its own road to the document, and it doesn't care which folder the document sits in. Ivan can't edit anything, though. The rule you wrote was about viewing, and nobody asked for more.

## The wall between companies

Globex has an IT admin too: Hana. Before you sleep tonight, you want to see with your own eyes that the wall between the two companies holds:

<include>../../../snippets/tutorial/05-company/other-company.ts</include>

The script prints:

```txt
hana can_view document:roadmap: denied
ivan can_view document:roadmap: allowed
```

Hana is an admin, but of Globex. The roadmap belongs to Acme, so the road through its organization leads to Acme's admins, and Hana isn't one of them. There's no `companyId` comparison anywhere in your code, and there never will be. The boundary between companies is built into the shape of the schema itself.

## What you built

"Admin" stopped being a label on a person and became a relation between a person and a company. That small shift is what makes the wall between Acme and Globex impossible to forget: an admin is always an admin _of_ something, and the schema always knows what.

The habit carried you through again. Ivan's role and each document's company were new facts. "A company's admins can view its documents" was the one new rule, and it slotted into `can_view` next to the folders, without disturbing anything else.
